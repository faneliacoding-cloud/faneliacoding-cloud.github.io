Wellness Tools

Self Care Tools

Practical, evidence-based techniques you can use anytime — at home, at work, or on the go.

Home›Resources›Self Care Tools
💚

These tools are meant to support your wellness between therapy sessions — they are not a replacement for professional care. If you're struggling, please reach out to us or call 988 if you're in crisis.

🌬️ Breathing
🌬️
Anxiety Relief

4-7-8 Breathing

This technique activates your parasympathetic nervous system, helping calm anxious thoughts and slow your heart rate within minutes.

  1. Exhale completely through your mouth
  2. Inhale through your nose for 4 counts
  3. Hold your breath for 7 counts
  4. Exhale slowly through your mouth for 8 counts
  5. Repeat 3–4 cycles
💡 Best for: Anxiety, panic, trouble falling asleep
🟦
Stress & Focus

Box Breathing

Used by Navy SEALs and athletes to regulate stress and sharpen focus. Equal counts create a calming rhythm.

  1. Inhale for 4 counts
  2. Hold for 4 counts
  3. Exhale for 4 counts
  4. Hold for 4 counts
  5. Repeat for 4–5 minutes
💡 Best for: Stress before meetings, presentations, or difficult conversations
🦁
Tension Release

Lion's Breath

A yoga-inspired breath that releases tension from the jaw, face, and chest — areas where we hold a lot of stress.

  1. Sit comfortably, inhale deeply through the nose
  2. Open your mouth wide, stick out your tongue
  3. Exhale forcefully with a "haaa" sound
  4. Repeat 3–5 times
💡 Best for: Physical tension, frustration, jaw-clenching
🌿 Grounding
✋
Anxiety & Dissociation

5-4-3-2-1 Technique

Brings you back to the present moment by engaging all five senses. Especially effective during anxiety or flashbacks.

  • 5 things you can see
  • 4 things you can touch — feel their texture
  • 3 things you can hear
  • 2 things you can smell
  • 1 thing you can taste
💡 Best for: Panic attacks, trauma responses, feeling "zoned out"
🦶
Mindfulness

Feet on the Floor

A simple but powerful body-awareness practice that anchors you to the present moment through physical sensation.

  1. Sit or stand with feet flat on the floor
  2. Press your feet firmly into the ground
  3. Notice the pressure, texture, temperature
  4. Take 3 slow, deep breaths
  5. Name 3 things you can see in the room
💡 Best for: Overwhelm, dissociation, transitions between activities
🧊
Intense Emotions

Temperature Grounding

Physical sensations from temperature rapidly interrupt distress cycles. Based on DBT's TIPP skill (Temperature, Intense Exercise, Paced Breathing, Paired Muscle Relaxation).

  • Hold an ice cube in your hand
  • Splash cold water on your face
  • Hold a warm mug of tea and breathe in the steam
  • Step outside for fresh air
💡 Best for: Overwhelming emotions, urges, breaking a rumination loop
📓 Journaling
✍️
Daily Practice

Morning Pages

Write 3 pages of stream-of-consciousness first thing in the morning before your inner critic wakes up. No editing, no judgment.

  • Set a timer: 15–20 minutes
  • Write without stopping or reading back
  • Don't worry about grammar or sense
  • Include frustrations, dreams, random thoughts
💡 Best for: Clearing mental clutter, creative blocks, processing overnight feelings
🌱
Prompts

Reflection Prompts

Use these when you don't know where to start:

  • What emotion am I carrying right now, and where do I feel it in my body?
  • What did I do today that I'm proud of, even if it was small?
  • What am I avoiding, and what is it costing me?
  • What would I tell a friend who was feeling exactly how I feel?
  • What does my version of "okay" look and feel like?
🙏
Positive Mindset

Gratitude Practice

Research shows consistent gratitude journaling rewires the brain toward positive attention patterns over time.

  • Write 3 specific things you're grateful for each day
  • Be specific: not "my family" but "my sister texted to check on me"
  • Include why it matters to you
  • Include one thing about yourself you appreciate
💡 Best for: Depression, low motivation, negativity bias
🧘 Movement & Body
💪
Stress Release

Progressive Muscle Relaxation

Tense and release each muscle group to release physical stress stored in the body. Takes about 10–15 minutes.

  1. Start with your feet — squeeze for 5 seconds, release
  2. Move up: calves, thighs, stomach, hands, arms, shoulders, face
  3. Notice the contrast between tension and relaxation
  4. Breathe slowly throughout
💡 Best for: Physical tension, chronic stress, trouble sleeping
🚶
Mindful Movement

Mindful Walking

Turn a regular walk into a mindfulness practice by bringing full attention to each step and sensation.

  • Walk without headphones or phone
  • Notice each foot lifting, moving, landing
  • Feel the air on your skin, sounds around you
  • When your mind wanders, gently return to your feet
  • Start with just 10 minutes
💡 Best for: Anxiety, depression, mental fatigue, rumination
🧘
Trauma-Informed

Body Scan Meditation

A gentle practice of noticing physical sensations without judgment. Helps reconnect mind and body.

  1. Lie down or sit comfortably
  2. Close your eyes and take 3 deep breaths
  3. Slowly scan from the top of your head to your toes
  4. Notice sensation: warmth, tension, tingling, numbness
  5. Don't try to change anything — just observe
💡 Best for: Trauma, disconnection from body, chronic pain, anxiety
🌙 Sleep Hygiene
🌙
Wind-Down Routine

The 10-3-2-1-0 Rule

  • 10 hours before bed: No more caffeine
  • 3 hours before bed: No more food or alcohol
  • 2 hours before bed: No more work
  • 1 hour before bed: No more screens
  • 0: The number of times you hit snooze
💡 Best for: Insomnia, anxiety at night, waking up exhausted
📵
Digital Wellness

Phone-Free Bedtime

Blue light suppresses melatonin, and doom-scrolling activates your stress response at exactly the wrong time.

  • Charge your phone outside the bedroom
  • Use a real alarm clock
  • Replace scrolling with reading, stretching, or journaling
  • Try a "digital sunset" 60–90 minutes before sleep
💡 Give it 2 weeks — most people see a noticeable difference in sleep quality
